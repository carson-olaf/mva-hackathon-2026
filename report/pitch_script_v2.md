# Pitch video narration (submitted)

Final narration of the Track 2 pitch video, https://youtu.be/DIucVnptRfM (2:55), Track 2 submission 3. Voice: ElevenLabs Matilda (AI-generated). Slides carry the source details listed in [pitch_script.md](pitch_script.md).

## Cold open

One child. One broken checkpoint gene. One drug already approved for children. And a lab test built to give a clear answer.

## Scene 1

In MVA, a child's cells share out chromosomes unevenly. That brings a high risk of childhood cancers, including rhabdomyosarcoma. Could a medicine children already take be worth testing?

## Scene 2

We found two changes in BUB1B, the cell's division referee. The first, L737*, cuts the protein short. The second, N1002K, swaps one amino acid. On opposite copies, they would leave no working BUB1B. We can't confirm that yet: no parental samples.

## Scene 3

Our candidate: Xuriden, uridine triacetate, a source of uridine, a DNA and RNA building block. It's FDA approved for adults and children, for a different rare disease, with at least two years of use in four patients. So a lab test is credible. Safety in this child is unknown.

## Scene 4

Our idea: stressed DNA copying damages chromosomes, and a weak referee lets them mis-split. Burrell and colleagues gave four unstable cancer lines a four-nucleoside mix. Segregation errors fell 45 to 55 percent. Xuriden's label: a mean uridine peak of 88.7 micromolar, about three times Burrell's nominal 30. A reference, not a dose target. An overlap, not proof it works.

## Scene 5

Now the honest part. That study used a mixture, with no uridine-only arm. No one has shown BUB1B errors respond to nucleotides. And another lab study saw uracil DNA damage at 10 or 30 micromolar uridine. So the dose that might help could also harm.

## Scene 6

We test this exact BUB1B genotype against corrected cells. Uridine alone, the full mix, or vehicle, at label-compatible exposures. We measure division errors, nucleotide balance, DNA damage and growth. Plus rhabdomyosarcoma models, so a yes or a no is decisive.

## Scene 7

We set the rules in advance. Uridine must cut division errors at least 30 percent, with a 95 percent interval excluding no benefit, and keep at least 90 percent of viable output. If it raises DNA damage or helps tumors grow, we stop. These are lab gates, not clinical predictions.

## Scene 8

Our open evidence engine keeps failed and contrary results, so anyone can audit it. Our census found 13,146 gene-ligand pairs tied to the wrong genes in a public database. That bug is now fixed upstream, for everyone.

## Scene 9

Uridine alone must improve chromosome fidelity at attainable exposure, without genomic or tumor harm. This earns an experiment, by a route reusable for other cases. It does not authorize treating the child.
