# Uridine triacetate for BUB1B-MVA: test rescue and cancer safety

Draft three-minute pitch. Script only; no video has been recorded. Slide cues and source notes are not spoken. Rehearse aloud and verify the recording's actual duration.

## Spoken script

[Slide 1 — The proposal and its kill test]

We propose testing oral uridine triacetate, Xuriden, to reduce replication stress and chromosome errors in BUB1B-MVA. Approved pediatric use and achievable uridine exposure make the experiment credible. Progression stops if uridine alone fails under the tested conditions, increases DNA damage or supports rhabdomyosarcoma.

[Slide 2 — A functional precedent, with two missing bridges]

Burrell and colleagues reduced segregation errors by forty-five to fifty-five percent in four unstable cancer cell lines after forty-eight hours of nucleoside supplementation. They supplied adenosine, cytidine, guanosine and uridine together.

We retrieved the supplement. It contains no uridine-only arm and cannot establish which components are necessary. Two transfers remain unproved: BUB1B errors must include a nucleotide-responsive component, and uridine alone must reproduce enough mixture benefit.

[Slide 3 — Exposure overlap does not establish efficacy]

Xuriden is FDA-approved oral granules for hereditary orotic aciduria in adults and children. Its label reports a mean plasma uridine peak of eighty-eight point seven micromolar in four patients: two point nine six times the nominal thirty-micromolar comparator in Burrell's methods.

Thirty micromolar is a reference concentration, not a uridine efficacy threshold. The ratio is not an efficacy margin or proof of adequate target coverage. A peak does not establish continuous exposure. The label documents at least twenty-four months of use in four patients. That supports chronic feasibility, while safety in this cancer-predisposition syndrome remains unknown.

[Slide 4 — The hazard sits in the same concentration range]

A separate study reported uracil-DNA damage at ten or thirty micromolar uridine in cancer cells. Those are laboratory findings, not a human toxicity threshold. They require testing harm beside rescue from the start.

[Slide 5 — One paired experiment decides progression]

In cells with the exact BUB1B genotype and corrected controls, compare uridine alone with the full mixture under measured exposure profiles compatible with the label. Correction establishes BUB1B dependence, not the correctable mechanism. Count errors per completed division, nucleotide balance, DNA damage and viable proliferative output. Run the same exposures in rhabdomyosarcoma models.

Our proposed laboratory gate is at least thirty percent fewer division errors, with an interval excluding no improvement, and at least ninety percent preserved viable output. Any reproducible increase in DNA damage or tumor fitness stops progression. These are experimental gates, not clinical benefit predictions.

[Slide 6 — A reusable route to a decisive answer]

Earlier routes are deprioritized under the available evidence, not generally disproved. Our collision census adds a target-identity check. The open engine preserves failed and contrary results.

Our contribution is a potentially novel hypothesis, a reproducible evidence audit and a mechanism-testing experiment. The next result we need is specific: uridine alone must improve chromosome fidelity at attainable exposure, with a separation from genomic and tumor harm. That earns further development. It does not authorize treating the child.

## Source notes — not spoken

- Mixture result and main-method concentration ambiguity: Burrell et al., **PMID 23446422; DOI 10.1038/nature11935**, [primary main text, Figure 4 and methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4636055/). The 45–55% reduction concerns four-component supplementation. No source found demonstrating uridine-alone rescue of this BUB1B genotype.
- Supplement obtained and reviewed: [publisher supplementary PDF, MOESM68](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fnature11935/MediaObjects/41586_2013_BFnature11935_MOESM68_ESM.pdf), PDF pages 13–14, Supplementary Figures 13–14. No single-component or component-removal arm was found. The exact 0.3-versus-30 µM assignment and explicit per-component interpretation remain unresolved. Supplementary retrieval scope and hash are recorded in the report's source audit.
- Product, approval, pediatric precedent and exposure: [official Xuriden label, §§1–2, 6.1, 12.3 Table 3 and 14](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=88ca4d2b-3905-474b-9fd6-c81474f2fb4b&type=display). Day 28 mean plasma uridine Cmax 88.7 µM, n=4. Arithmetic: 88.7 / 30 = 2.9567, rounded to 2.96. This is a nominal comparison, not an efficacy margin or patient-specific exposure. The label and regulatory records have no article PMID/DOI.
- Hazard: Cao et al., **PMID 26801745; DOI 10.1016/j.canlet.2016.01.007**, [opened primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26801745/). Reported 10/30 µM cell-culture findings are not human toxicity thresholds. Full figure-level exposure duration remains unverified.
- Balanced chronic safety evidence and mechanism limits: [uridine decision report](nucleoside_decision.md) and [source audit](../results/nucleoside_source_audit.json). The hypothesis does not require mutant-protein instability, a druggable pocket, an expression signature, a modifier or detectable bulk blood aneuploidy.

The full protocol requires independent confirmation and tumor-safety precision; no clinical safety claim follows from passing a cell assay.

The modeled period excludes the rising limb and is not total daily time above the 30 µM reference. [Arithmetic and limitations](nucleoside_decision.md).

- Transfer limit: Burrell reports no observed checkpoint-function difference between CIN groups and no nucleoside response in 18q-normal CIN-positive NCIH508. Main text before Figures 1 and 4; DOI [10.1038/nature11935](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4636055/). Neither observation tests BUB1B-MVA.
