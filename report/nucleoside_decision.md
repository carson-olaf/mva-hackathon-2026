# Uridine triacetate: peak uridine exposure is feasible; the therapeutic window remains unproved

24 October 2026. Focused follow-up authorized after the earlier analysis cutoff. Research hypothesis, not medical advice. No clinician has reviewed or signed this assessment. No patient data were downloaded or reanalysed.

**Decision: retain Xuriden for a decisive laboratory test. Do not nominate years-long treatment in this child.** The earlier 255-fold comparison concerned Kygevvi's thymidine and cannot exclude uridine triacetate. Oral Xuriden reaches plasma uridine concentrations above the higher number listed in Burrell's methods. However, neither uridine-alone rescue of BUB1B-MVA nor an oncology-safe exposure window is established. There is no defensible number proving the approach impossible. There is also no defensible number establishing its safety.

**What must transfer from Burrell.** The study used adenosine, cytidine, guanosine and uridine together. After 48 hours, segregation errors fell 45–55% in HT29, HT55, SW620 and SW1116; measured proliferation and cell-cycle distribution did not change. Methods list 0.3 or 30 µM. The opened main text does not resolve which concentration produced each Figure 4 endpoint, or explicitly settle the per-component interpretation. The subsequent focused check retrieved the actual publisher supplement: its Figures 13–14 add no component-specific arm or dose assignment. It establishes neither uridine-alone sufficiency nor four-component necessity. See the [supplement audit](../results/burrell_supplement_audit.md). No uridine-alone arm or minimum effective concentration was verified. Consequently, **30 µM below is a nominal comparator, not an established uridine-alone rescue threshold**. [Burrell et al., PMID 23446422; DOI 10.1038/nature11935, Figure 4 and cell-treatment methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4636055/).

**The exposure arithmetic.** Xuriden is FDA-approved oral uridine triacetate granules for adult and pediatric hereditary orotic aciduria. Its label describes 60 mg/kg once daily, with escalation to 120 mg/kg, maximum 8 g, for inadequate efficacy. These are published regimens, not a dose recommendation for this child. The measured analyte is plasma **uridine**, not the prodrug. [Official label, §§1–2 and 12.3, Table 3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=88ca4d2b-3905-474b-9fd6-c81474f2fb4b&type=display).

| Label observation | Day 28, 60 mg/kg/day | Day 160, 120 mg/kg/day |
|---|---:|---:|
| Participants | 4 | 2 |
| Uridine peak, mean ± SD | 88.7 ± 43.2 µM | 80.9 ± 20.0 µM |
| AUC0–8, mean ± SD | 278.7 ± 148.5 µM·h | 465.6 ± 95.3 µM·h |
| Half-life, mean ± SD | 2.3 ± 1.6 h | 8.2 ± 6.8 h |
| Calculated peak / 30 µM | **2.96** | **2.70** |
| Calculated AUC0–8 / 8 h | **34.84 µM** | **58.20 µM** |

The calculations use the label's native units. No molecular-weight conversion is needed. The eight-hour averages are **not daily averages**. Small, partly different groups cannot establish dose proportionality. The label provides no validated 24-hour trough, unbound exposure or intracellular nucleotide concentrations. The child's exposure cannot be inferred from these means.

For sensitivity only, a baseline-aware exponential using the Day 28 mean peak and half-life crosses the **30 µM reference concentration** approximately **3.83–4.01 hours after the peak**, assuming a baseline of 3–5 µM. Roughly four hours is a modeled **post-peak period**, not total daily time above the comparator: it ignores the rising limb. The 3.83–4.01-hour range varies only the assumed baseline; it is **not an uncertainty interval over peak or half-life variability**. Those baselines are assumptions, not observations in this child. The corresponding Day 160 calculation is 12.54–13.14 hours; that range also varies only baseline and does not capture the especially large PK variability. These are not fitted individual profiles or measured daily exposure durations. They omit absorption, distribution and intracellular persistence. Thirty µM is not an established uridine efficacy threshold, and the 2.96 peak ratio is not a threefold efficacy margin, adequate target coverage or an established therapeutic window. The calculations cannot prove or disprove that a brief daily pulse gives durable cellular benefit. Formula and exact inputs are in the reproducible audit below.

Burrell also reports no observed mitotic-checkpoint difference between CIN groups, and no supplementation response in the 18q-normal CIN-positive NCIH508 line. These findings limit transfer to BUB1B; they do not establish that this genotype is unresponsive. [Primary main text, Figures 1/4 context and Supplementary Figures 2/13](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4636055/).

**The geneticist's unresolved question is responsiveness, not protein stability.** BubR1 acetylation-defective mouse K243R/+ cells show replication stress and chromosome rearrangements. That is a distinct allele and species; it does not show nucleotide shortage or uridine rescue in this child. [Park et al., PMID 33955658; DOI 10.1111/febs.15912](https://febs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/febs.15912).

The proposed mechanism is therefore a **hypothesis**: uridine could reduce a secondary, nucleotide-responsive replication burden while the primary checkpoint defect remains. **No source found** demonstrating this mechanism in the exact genotype, explaining its congenital nephrocalcinosis, or showing that uridine alone can replace all four supplied nucleosides. It requires none of the reported findings from the eight deprioritized routes to be wrong. Inconclusive screens do not exclude future benefit from those routes. It requires a new, measurable response in cells with the exact genotype.

**Metabolic counterevidence:** Pandey et al. found mainly CTP/UTP increases, modest dCTP changes and little fork acceleration with ribonucleosides; thymidine-containing conditions had stronger effects. This motivates direct pool and fork measurements, not an inference of uridine efficacy from plasma concentration. [Same paper already cited in this repository, DOI 10.1093/nar/gkaf1035](https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:2010300/FULLTEXT01.pdf).

**Cancer safety blocks a clinical nomination.** The evidence points in both directions:

- **A concentration-relevant hazard:** Cao and colleagues report uracil-DNA damage and damage signaling at **10 or 30 µM uridine** in four cancer cell lines. Uridine-phosphorylase knockout also increased spontaneous tumors more than threefold in mice. The former overlaps the exposure under consideration; the latter is a genetic catabolism defect, not oral Xuriden treatment. Neither supplies a human toxicity threshold, a recurrence-risk estimate or a clinical therapeutic index. The opened abstract supports these findings; full figures and exposure durations were not verified. [PMID 26801745; DOI 10.1016/j.canlet.2016.01.007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26801745/).
- **Real, limited reassurance:** the Xuriden label reports four patients aged 3–19 treated for at least 24 months without reported adverse reactions, and negative standard Ames, mouse-lymphoma and mouse-micronucleus tests. Formal long-term animal carcinogenicity studies were not performed. [Label, §§6.1 and 13.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=88ca4d2b-3905-474b-9fd6-c81474f2fb4b&type=display). The FDA review also cites experience extending to 19 years without reported neoplasia, but supplies no usable denominator or formal surveillance design for that statement. Its broader nonclinical review did not identify treatment-related immunosuppression or a neoplastic pattern; it recommended a carcinogenicity-study waiver. This is meaningful evidence, not proof of safety in a cancer-predisposition syndrome. [FDA pharmacology review, printed pp51–54 and 66; Reference ID 3781495](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2015/208169Orig1s000PharmR.pdf).
- **Tumor fuel is a plausible competing outcome:** uridine-derived ribose supported glucose-restricted pancreatic cancer growth, with UPP1 dependence. This is a different tumor, not evidence that Xuriden feeds rhabdomyosarcoma in patients. [Nwosu et al., PMID 37198494; DOI 10.1038/s41586-023-06073-w](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06073-w). A recent PAX3–FOXO1 RMS study also found pyrimidine-dependent metabolic vulnerabilities, but a uridine-alone rescue result was not verified. The child's tumor fusion status is unknown. [Paras et al., PMID 40911784; DOI 10.1158/0008-5472.CAN-25-0315](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40911784/).
- **The net immune effect cannot be assumed:** another study found that uridine supplementation improved CD8 T-cell antitumor activity in mouse models. This counters a blanket claim that supplementation necessarily promotes cancer, without establishing this child's safety. [Xiao et al., PMID 41468885; DOI 10.1016/j.cmet.2025.11.016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41468885/).

The relevant risk is not just a larger tumor. Extra uridine could change DNA damage, clonal survival, treatment sensitivity or immune surveillance. Conversely, correcting replication stress might reduce new errors. **No source found** resolving that balance for chronic uridine triacetate in BUB1B-MVA. Low blood aneuploidy does not answer it. No individual renal function, current oncology status, medicines or PK measurements were supplied.

Vistogard, the same active drug's acute rescue product, is FDA-approved for fluorouracil/capecitabine overdose or early severe toxicity. Its label warns that non-emergency use can diminish chemotherapy efficacy. Its short-course experience cannot justify years of frequent dosing to maintain uridine. This is a verified fluoropyrimidine interaction, not a claim about this child's treatment regimen. [Official Vistogard label, §§1 and 6.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=269f7363-63ed-444e-85f1-f0009e44818b&type=display).

**The experiment: a paired uridine-only rescue and harm test.** This is a proposed design, not completed work or a validated clinical-benefit threshold.

1. Use nonmalignant cells engineered with the exact two BUB1B alleles in trans, two independent clones and corrected isogenic controls. Authorized patient cells could add validation if already available. First require a division-error defect that correction reverses; otherwise the assay cannot adjudicate rescue. This establishes BUB1B dependence, not replication stress as the correctable cause. Include an external HT29 control to test the published nucleoside-responsive setting.
2. Compare vehicle, uridine alone and the four-nucleoside mixture over 48 hours. Test defined equal-molar mixture benchmarks at 0.3 and 30 µM **per component as our experimental choices**, not a claimed exact reconstruction of the unresolved original dose assignment. Measure baseline nucleosides in medium. In separate uridine-only arms, reproduce concentration-time profiles compatible with the reported peaks and first-eight-hour AUCs, bracket the unknown overnight tail, and measure actual medium exposure. Measure intracellular UTP/CTP, all four canonical dNTPs and dUTP, including the dUTP/dTTP ratio. Measure fork progression directly with a DNA-fiber assay rather than infer replication benefit from extracellular concentration. Do not label these profiles patient-matched. A constant 30 µM arm is a mechanistic comparator, not proof of clinically feasible continuous exposure.
3. Count errors **per completed division**, blinded, distinguishing centromere-positive laggards from acentric fragments and bridges. Track viable proliferative output, arrest and cell death. Add uracil-sensitive DNA-damage measurements, such as a validated UDG-dependent assay, with γH2AX as a complementary readout. A lower abnormal-cell fraction caused by arrest or killing is not rescue.
4. Run the same measured exposure profiles in fusion-positive and fusion-negative RMS models, under nutrient-replete and glucose-restricted conditions. Measure direct cell counts and clonogenic survival through a 10–14-day follow-up; ATP alone is insufficient for a metabolic intervention. These models bracket risks and do not represent the child's unknown tumor subtype. Tumor-only cultures cannot establish immune safety.

**Prespecified laboratory gate:** select one exposure in an exploratory run, then confirm independently. Require at least **30% relative reduction in division errors**, a 95% interval excluding no benefit, and at least **90% of vehicle viable proliferative output**. Require no reproducible increase in uracil-DNA damage or adverse clonal selection. Any reproducible RMS fitness increase fails, even if smaller than 20%. Also require sufficient precision: for RMS clonogenic output, provisionally require the upper one-sided 95% confidence bound on the treated/vehicle ratio to be **below 1.20**. This 20% harmful-effect margin is a proposed screening choice that an oncologist must review, not permission for a demonstrated smaller increase or an acceptable clinical cancer-risk increase. Power from between-culture pilot variability; cells in a dish are not independent biological replicates. A wide interval is inconclusive, not a safety pass.

If the mixture works but uridine alone fails at the exposure profiles tested, the approved-product substitution fails under those tested conditions. If neither works while the external positive control does, the proposed responsive component is not demonstrated under the tested conditions. If uridine improves division fidelity while increasing DNA damage or RMS fitness, it fails the chronic-use screen. A pass justifies replication, longer exposure, relevant tissues, immune-competent cancer-risk work and clinical pharmacology review. **It does not authorize treating the child.**

The single missing result is **uridine-alone functional rescue at attainable exposure with a demonstrable separation from genomic and tumor harm**. Once suitable cells exist, the paired experiment is the shortest defensible route to that result. Cell access and engineering remain prerequisites; no cost or completion date is invented.
