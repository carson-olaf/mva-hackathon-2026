The most informative new positive experiments concerned two ways to reduce the burden on chromosome segregation: reduce excessive kinetochore signaling, or reduce replication-associated damage. Both have real laboratory support. Neither currently supplies an approved intervention that restores this genotype's fidelity at a credible chronic pediatric exposure.

## Reducing the attachment burden

Herman and colleagues found a direct functional bypass in RAS-transformed ARPE19 cells: 10 µM U0126 for one hour restored alignment in over 90% of BubR1-depleted cells. However, the assay also used MG132 to prevent mitotic exit. The paper's separate live-imaging result—20/522 erroneous divisions versus 3/250 with U0126—used RAS-transformed cells with BubR1 present. Combining those experiments into “BubR1-deficient division rescue” would be incorrect. U0126 is a research compound. [PMID 36191188; DOI 10.1073/pnas.2208255119](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36191188/). Figures 1G and 5C were inspected in the primary full paper by the research reviewer; bibliographic identity and abstract were independently checked.

An approved MEK inhibitor cannot inherit that result without its own experiment. Selumetinib has FDA approval for NF1 with symptomatic inoperable plexiform neurofibromas, including children from age one, with oral granules available. Its label reports cardiac, ocular and muscle toxicities, and a high-exposure mouse micronucleus signal. These findings do not prove clinical-dose aneuploidy, but they prevent assuming genomic safety in MVA. [Official Koselugo label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=7d042c61-f28f-4ab5-ab10-d7558c0d49ff). Separately, chronic selumetinib exposure produced cell-cycle escape and copy-number changes in a BRAF-mutant colorectal model. [PMID 36267209; DOI 10.1093/narcan/zcac032](https://academic.oup.com/narcancer/article/4/4/zcac032/6761901).

**No source found** showing excessive MAPK-dependent kinetochore signaling in these exact BUB1B products, approved-MEK-drug fidelity rescue in BUB1B-MVA, or a renal-calcium explanation from this route. It requires none of the eight negative analyses to be wrong; it fails independently at the disease-state, drug and safety bridges. Do not add selumetinib to the treatment shortlist from this evidence.

## Reducing replication-associated damage

Burrell and colleagues actually reduced segregation errors by 45–55% in four CIN-positive colorectal cancer lines using 48-hour nucleoside supplementation. The mixture was **adenosine, cytidine, guanosine and uridine**, with methods listing 0.3 or 30 µM. It did not alter measured proliferation or cell-cycle distribution. This is stronger than a gene-expression correlation, but it is neither a BUB1B experiment nor a result for every nucleoside mixture. [PMID 23446422; DOI 10.1038/nature11935](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4636055/), Figure 4 and cell-treatment methods.

The recently approved product Kygevvi instead contains **deoxycytidine and thymidine**. FDA approval covers TK2 deficiency with symptom onset at or before age 12, in adults and children; an oral-solution powder is available. Its mean total-plasma thymidine peak is 19 ng/mL. With molecular weight 242.23, this is **0.0784 µM**. A separate 2025 experiment used **20 µM thymidine** to accelerate replication forks—about **255 times** that peak—and did not establish completed-division rescue. Plasma and culture concentrations are not interchangeable; this arithmetic identifies an unresolved exposure gap, not a dose-escalation proposal. [FDA label, §§1, 11–13](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2025/219792s000lbl.pdf), [primary fork experiment, DOI 10.1093/nar/gkaf1035](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/53/19/gkaf1035/8285779).

The same FDA label reports chromosome aberrations in cultured human lymphocytes, negative Ames/rat micronucleus results for the active combination, and no animal carcinogenicity studies. It does not establish clinical cancer harm, but rules out describing this as automatically harmless replacement. **No source found** for BUB1B fidelity rescue by the approved pair at realistic exposure. No renal-mechanism bridge was found. The mixture, endpoint and exposure mismatches prevent a repurposing claim for **Kygevvi**. The separate [Xuriden analysis](nucleoside_decision.md) supersedes any broader nucleoside-class exclusion.

## A recent mitochondrial lead does not close the gap

Elamipretide (SS-31) reduced aneuploidy and improved mitochondrial measures in aged mouse oocytes. This is a genuine functional result, but it concerns reproductive aging rather than genetic BubR1 deficiency. [PMID 41612464; DOI 10.1186/s13048-026-01975-5](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12930897/). Forzinity has FDA accelerated approval to improve muscle strength in adults and children with Barth syndrome weighing at least 30 kg, with daily subcutaneous administration. The label reports negative mutagenicity/chromosome-damage assays, no carcinogenicity studies, and insufficient information to recommend pediatric dosing with renal impairment. [Official label, §§1, 2, 13](https://www.dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=146bf34c-76f2-48db-ac07-fb29cce2cd75&type=display).

**No source found** demonstrating elamipretide rescue of BUB1B-deficient division, a relevant mitochondrial defect caused by N1002K, or a BUB1B–mitochondrial–nephrocalcinosis pathway. Approval and a positive oocyte experiment do not supply those missing links. It is not nominated as a new MVA treatment; its genomic assays also do not establish long-term safety in a child with prior RMS.

## Decision boundary

None of these leads has the required positive link from the exact disease state to useful functional rescue by an eligible drug. A faster fork, aligned metaphase plate, smaller tumor, or lower surviving aneuploid fraction is insufficient by itself. Proposed experiments must distinguish improved division from arrest or selective killing and must measure structural chromosome damage and tumor-relevant effects.

The new findings therefore receive **zero positive BUB1B drug-rescue lines**. No clinical candidate is declared. The existing exact-product replacement experiment remains a valid way to obtain missing functional evidence, but it is not presented as a new treatment or as completion of the renewed goal. No claim is made that these branches explain congenital nephrocalcinosis or the unreplicated chr8p contrast.
