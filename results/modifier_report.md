# Modifier/second-hit research screen

Scanned 49/49 prespecified checkpoint/CIN, rhabdomyosarcoma and nephrocalcinosis genes locally. Found 33 protein-altering canonical-CDS calls including 2 BUB1B retrieval controls. Among the 31 remaining coding calls, 31 have returned population AF>0.001, 0 AF<=0.001 and 0 unknown frequency. Annotated a bounded shortlist of 12 named candidates externally. **No rare coding candidate was found where frequency is known; no variant is established as a BUB1B-MVA modifier.**

The 31 non-BUB1B calls all have returned population AF>0.001. Individual calls are withheld from this public repository to protect the child's privacy.

QC and translation reuse Track1. Canonical coding exons only; gene-level phase, splice regions, fusions (including PAX3/PAX7-FOXO1), deletions/CNV and tumor-specific second hits are not assessed. This VCF is not a paired tumor-normal study. Rarity is confirmed only where a population frequency is returned; unknown is not rare. External candidates are ordered by truncating/in-frame/missense impact, then gene/coordinate. Up to twelve named variants are sent to VEP. Local exact-allele ClinVar AF_EXAC/AF_TGP fields additionally exclude common alleles throughout the panel; these are legacy ExAC/1000G frequencies, not contemporary gnomAD absence evidence. For residual unknowns, at most five bounded public gnomAD gene references can be queried by gene identifier only, then matched locally: no additional patient variant is sent. Absence in the public gene response remains unknown. Unknown/rare candidates appear first in the publication shortlist; common calls are controls. The full aggregate rarity counts and public-reference hashes/queries are in modifier_summary.json; remaining calls stay private. SLC9A3R1 was resolved through its current HGNC name NHERF1 (NCBI Gene9368).

Null/control: one genome cannot establish modifier enrichment or penetrance. No population enrichment inference or null p-value is reported. BUB1B is an internal retrieval control only; common variants in the shortlist are deprioritization controls, not disease-causing discoveries. Functional causality needs isogenic rescue/perturbation and segregation.
