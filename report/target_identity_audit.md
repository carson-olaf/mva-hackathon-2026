# Target-identity audit: a false BUB1B drug lead

24 October 2026. Public-source audit; no patient data. This finding improves the reliability of repurposing, but does not identify a treatment.

**Niflumic acid must not receive a BUB1B-activation evidence score from the records examined here.** The experimental source studies renal chloride channels. The subsequent [public collision census](collision_audit.md) established the faulty DGIdb importer line and reproduced 13,146 historical wrong gene–GtoP-ligand pairs across 1,403 genes. DGIdb already repaired that importer in [PR624](https://github.com/dgidb/dgidb-v5/pull/624), merged 14 April 2026. The exact numeric-swap class is absent from the tested June and October 2026 exports; 11 other identity conflicts remain. This updates the original case audit's uncertainty about the point of failure.

A 2026 gentamicin-nephrotoxicity analysis lists niflumic acid as a BUB1B activator, together with 3-phenyl-CPP and DIDS as BUB1B inhibitors. Its table also associates lubiprostone, GaTx2 and diphenylamine-2-carboxylic acid with BUB1. These are database-derived predictions, not drug-target experiments in that paper. [Primary analysis, Table 3; DOI 10.1007/s00210-026-04997-4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13152955/).

The underlying identifiers distinguish two entirely different targets. **IUPHAR target 701 is ClC-Kb/CLCNKB**, while **NCBI Entrez Gene 701 is BUB1B**. The actual IUPHAR BUB1B record is **target 1950**, which links to Entrez 701 and UniProt O60566. The second pair provides an independent cross-check: **IUPHAR target 699 is ClC-2/CLCN2**, with Entrez 1181; Entrez 699 instead denotes BUB1. The channel drug sets match the sets assigned to the checkpoint genes. [Official ClC-family record](https://www.guidetomalariapharmacology.org/GRAC/FamilyDisplayForward?familyId=128&objId=703), [official BUB1B record](https://www.guidetopharmacology.org/GRAC/ObjectDisplayForward?objectId=1950), [official ClC-2 record](https://www.guidetopharmacology.org/GRAC/ObjectDisplayForward?objectId=699). Database records have no article PMID/DOI. Some direct requests redirected to login; indexed official record text was inspected, and this access limitation is retained.

The primary pharmacology experiment removes the biological ambiguity. Liantonio and colleagues expressed CLC-K channels in Xenopus oocytes and measured chloride currents by voltage clamp. Niflumic acid increased CLC-Ka and CLC-Kb currents at 10–1000 µM. The experiment does not test BubR1 or establish BUB1B activation. [PMID 16244177; DOI 10.1124/mol.105.017384](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16244177/). The primary abstract was read through indexed PubMed and the publisher's abstract; no unviewed figure is used.

The [subsequent census and upstream patch](collision_audit.md) establish the namespace mix-up in DGIdb's historical GtoP importer. The current live BUB1B API query no longer returns the three channel-drug associations. This does not prove that niflumic acid could never affect BubR1 by another mechanism. **No source found** for the asserted direct BUB1B-activation bridge. The claim receives zero support from the cited channel experiment.

For a reusable engine, an identifier is the pair `(namespace, value)`, not the bare number. A target assertion should retain source target ID, species, gene symbol, cross-referenced gene/protein accession, primary assay and assay direction. Conflicting identities must stop that evidence edge before ranking or docking. Agreement between several downstream sites is not independent evidence when they inherit the same mapping. The present `mva_repurpose` discovery path uses Open Targets, ChEMBL and Reactome; it does not ingest this DGIdb-derived edge, so no existing candidate score required correction.

No drug was administered, proposed for clinical use, downloaded, pushed, submitted or reported externally. AI: OpenAI; Pro; model training and data sharing disabled, as Carson confirmed.
